# Recursion: an EVM verifier for the Keccak VM

A proof of a Keccak VM execution (leanVM `9d50c92a3`'s tables, `docs/leanvm/univariate/KECCAK.md`) verified on the EVM
by recursion: π1 is the univariate proof, π2 a SNARK of π1's verifier, and the contract runs π2's verifier only. Code:
`crates/leanvm/rec`; π2's PIOP in [`PI2.md`](PI2.md); π1's Fiat–Shamir shape (π1-rec4, the paired batch) in
[`docs/leanvm/univariate/SPEC.md`](../univariate/SPEC.md); superseded designs and figures in
[`docs/HISTORY.md`](../../HISTORY.md), "leanVM".

Numbers are **measured** by `crates/leanvm/rec`'s tests (§10, §11) on the committed real proofs or the recorded
verifier, or **modelled** by `params/leanvm_rec_params.py` (`python3 -I`) from the measured counts (§3, §5).

```sh
export RUSTFLAGS="-C target-cpu=native"
cargo test --release -p leanvm_rec -- --nocapture                                            # circuit, PIOP, contract
cargo test --release -p leanvm_rec --test keccak_vm_rec -- --ignored --nocapture             # π1-rec4 under π2 at 128 and 400 (§1.2, §10.3)
cargo test --release -p leanvm_rec --test counts -- --include-ignored --nocapture            # π1's graph, its counts (§1)
cargo test --release -p leanvm_rec --test pi2_real -- --ignored --nocapture real_end_to_end  # the real proof end to end (§11)
```

## 0. Summary

- **π1** is the univariate PIOP of `crates/leanvm/univariate` on the Keccak VM, with the Fiat–Shamir shape **π1-rec4**
  (`leanvm_rec::PI1 = KECCAK_REC4`, `FsShape::Rec4`): the bus's `α` and Flock's zerocheck eq point are powers of one
  challenge each (§1.4); the batching sumcheck takes two variables a round with narrow (160-bit) challenges (§12); the
  challenges no message separates are drawn from one Keccak-f, and the ring map after `C2` (§1.3). Its statement and
  commitments are the main line's (`KECCAK_MAIN`). Its claims are 3 point claims (`C1`, `C2`, setup `F`) at one point
  generated by 14 narrow challenges. π1-rec3 (`KECCAK_REC3`, without the wide draws) is the inner proof the Lean states.
- **π2** is a univariate quotient SNARK, in the family of `crates/membership/quotient`. It proves that π1's verifier,
  without its PCS openings, accepts and outputs its PCS claims, and computes their Keccak-256s. Its arithmetization is
  **D16v** (§2.4): one copy argument in which a position's **push is a degree-2 expression of its row** (a "virtual
  push"); rows of 16 positions; Keccak-f in 130,944 positions (126,848 when only the digest is read); `E` in `K`-limbs.
- **The contract** reads π1's three claims' hashes from the frame of PCS claims (payloads 0 … 2) into π2's seed, runs
  π2's verifier, and checks π2's five claims declared (payloads 3 … 7).

| π1-rec4's verifier: Keccak-256 calls / Keccak-f / stream | 60 / 106 / 9,280 B | 61 / 108 / 9,472 B |
| π2's positions (D16v, `N = 2^24`, the three claims hashed) | 16,413,312 (97.8%) | 16,684,592 (99.4%) |
| calldata (zero bytes) | 577 B (4) | 577 B (5) |

π1's main line on chain without recursion takes 8,069 B and 231,511 execution at 128 signatures
(`docs/leanvm/univariate/KECCAK.md`). It does not fit π2: at 32 signatures it already needs `N = 2^25` (§2.4.5), whose
quotient table exceeds WHIR's `2^28` words. Hence π1-rec4.

## 1. π1's verifier, measured

### 1.1 Method

The univariate verifier, run on the recorder's symbol (`leanvm_statement::evm::record`), is a hash-consed graph: the
stream's reads; the Keccak-256 chain (`Hash(state, from, to)`, one Keccak-256 of the 32-byte state and the stream's
bytes `from..to`; `Low`, its low 192 bits; `Narrow`, its low 160; `Wide(hash, k)`, challenge `k` of the sponge's rate
after its last permutation); `E`'s `Add`, `Mul` and constants; bit fields of the row counts; the ring switch's kernel;
the checks; the claims' words. This graph is the circuit π2 proves.

- **`src/count.rs`** counts the **live** nodes (those the calls or the checks read). The ring kernel is expanded by
  recording `ring_tower::weight_at` on fresh symbols.
- **`src/eval.rs`** evaluates every node on a real proof. `tests/counts.rs`, `the_graph_is_the_verifier_on_real_proofs`:
  on the committed vectors (`keccak.txt`, the main line at 32 signatures; `keccak128_rec3`, `keccak_rec3`,
  `keccak128_rec4`, `keccak_rec4` at 128 and 400), the graph writes exactly the vectors' claims, every check holds, and
  a flipped stream byte breaks them. This is the reference π2's witness is checked against.

`E` operations at a skip of 3), so the arithmetic is small next to the Keccak-f.

### 1.3 The transcript

π1-rec4's calls (`tests/counts.rs`, `transcript_shape`; the derivation and its soundness:
`docs/leanvm/univariate/SPEC.md`, π1-rec4):

1. the seed: name, the contract's IV, the public input (80 B, no state);
2. the row counts, `C1`'s root and out-of-domain answer; one wide draw: `α`'s `c`, `β`, `γ`, the zerocheck's `c`;
3. Flock: the skip's coset values, the zerocheck's and the lincheck's rounds, one challenge each;
4. the slices, `C2`'s root and answer; one wide draw: `t` and the ring map's `f_0 … f_3`;
5. the values at `t`; one wide draw: `ρ`, the batch's `λ`, `f_4`, `f_5`;
6. the batching sumcheck: one narrow challenge a pair of variables (at 27 variables 13 pairs and one plain round).

A drawn challenge costs a permutation unless it is another of a wide draw (up to five, `WIDE_MAX`); data costs one
more per 136 bytes (a pair's 8 scalars and the state are two blocks). 106 − 60 = 46 permutations are later blocks. Any
sponge that absorbs every message before the challenge after it needs 105 on this stream.

### 1.4 The derived challenges

**π1-rec3 and π1-rec4 derive `α_2, α_3, α_4` from `α_1`, and Flock's zerocheck coordinates after its fixed ones from
the first, each the square of the one before** (`FsShape::draw`; `flock_min::zerocheck`; the prover's
`flock::zerocheck::skip::EqPoint::Squares`, and `α` squared in `E` as the verifier does).

- **The zerocheck's eq point.** `Σ_x eq(r, x) P(x)` is multilinear in each `r_i`, so `r_i = c^(2^i)` (a base-2
  Kronecker substitution) maps distinct monomials to distinct powers of `c`: the polynomial stays nonzero when `P ≠ 0`
  on the cube, of degree `2^n − 1` in `c` over `n` derived coordinates. The error goes from `n/|E|` to `2^n/|E|`.
- **The bus's `α`.** Each fingerprint `Σ_s eq(α, s) τ_s` is multilinear in `α`, so with `α = (c, c², c⁴, c⁸)` it is a
  polynomial `f_τ(c)` of degree ≤ 15; distinct tuples give distinct `f_τ` (the 16 eq polynomials at the power point
  are a basis of the polynomials of degree < 16). `β` stays independent. `Π(β + f_τ(c))` as polynomials in `β` over
  `E[c]` are equal only if the multisets `{f_τ}` are (the factors are irreducible in `β`): Schwartz–Zippel over
  `(β, c)` at degree 16 a factor.
- **Not derived:** `β` from `α` (products of the monic `c^16 + f_τ(c)` can coincide for different multisets); the ring
  map's `f_0..f_5` (the tower argument needs an independent `f_j` a level); `γ` (drawn in `α`'s wide draw, free).
- **Not helpful:** a SHAKE-like squeeze (the constraint is Keccak-256's chain; the wide draw reads the rate it already
  computes); larger skips (the coset values cost blocks); merging sumchecks (their rounds are one permutation each, and
  the lincheck cannot join the batch).

### 1.5 The claims

The three claims, on `C1`, `C2` and `F`, are at the batching sumcheck's point `r`, each at `r`'s prefix of its
commitment's variables (`verifier::batch`): 27 variables at the reference shape, 28 at 400 signatures. The Keccak VM
never commits Flock's matrices `A0`, `B0`. `r` is generated by 14 narrow challenges `z_j` (the paired batch). π2's
circuit derives the coordinates, encodes each claim (`version ‖ n ‖ root ‖ ood ‖ point ‖ value`, `82 + 24 n` bytes) and
hashes it (§2.4.6): nothing of π1's claims is in calldata. A claim the contract encodes instead (the fallback) carries
its distinct elements, which cannot be fewer: sumcheck challenges are sequential (a point derived from one seed lets
the prover know them in advance), and `q_flock`'s ring switch needs a fresh sumcheck over its variables (DEEP, Gemini,
Zeromorph and a univariate batch give the coordinates back as values).

## 2. π2's arithmetization

### 2.1 The reference: `crates/membership/quotient`

One gate `V = Y + C + X·C` on bits (a χ, an XOR, a NOT), one logarithmic-derivative copy argument with unique tags on
both sides (sound in characteristic 2, both sides being sets), one quotient identity at `t`. Measured at the design's
time: **113,114 rows** a Keccak-f (one Keccak-256 of a 64-byte block); the Merkle path's 34 permutations 3,878,615 of
`2^22` rows; the quotient 13.75 words a cell without hiding (`h` 3, `q` 9, gate 1.75). π2 needs no zero knowledge: π1
has none, and π2's witness is π1's proof, which is not secret.

### 2.2 Why bit-level wiring does not fit

With that gate π1's 106 permutations alone need 106 × 113,114 = 12.0M rows (`2^23.5`): at the 63 words a row of the
membership table it was measured on, a `2^29.5`-word quotient table, 2.8× over WHIR's `2^28`. The copy argument costs
about 9 words a wired cell (`h` 3, `q` 6, in `E`), whatever the gate, so what matters is the wired cells a Keccak round
needs: about 18,900 for the XOR-per-row circuit; about 3,840 at best with cell outputs (every state bit written once
and read once across ρπ's rotations, plus θ's parities). Rotations cannot be algebraic in characteristic 2
(`z ↦ z + r mod 64` is not GF(2)-affine, no affine map of `GF(2)^6` has order 64, and no element has multiplicative
order 64), so ρ is always wiring. On the other side, every relation the verifier checks at `t` among `L` cells costs
`L` values of calldata, or a sumcheck.

### 2.3 Virtual pushes

**The copy argument carries the computation.** Every position `i` of the witness `w` (a `K`-word, `N = 2^m`
positions on the Cantor subspace `V_m`) has:

- a **pull** `(w(ω_i), ω_i)`: its value, tagged with its own point;
- a **push** `(P(ω_i), σ(ω_i))`: a value sent to the position `σ(ω_i)`, a fixed permutation of the positions (setup),
  or to a tag outside `V_m` (§2.4.6).

`P(ω_i)` is a **forward** (its own pull, `P = w`), an **expression slot** (a polynomial `E_{b,k}` of degree ≤ 2 in the
16 values of its row, chosen by the position's **role** `k`, its low 4 index bits, and its row's **region block** `b`,
an aligned block of rows), or a **constant** `κ(ω_i)` (a setup column, zero off the constant positions). So
`P = w + Σ_(b,k) I_(b,k)·(E_(b,k) − w) + κ`, `I_(b,k)` the indicator of the positions of role `k` in block `b`.

That set is a coset of `U_s = span(β_4, …, β_(3+s))`, for a block of `2^s` rows. So
`I_(b,k)(x) = w_m(x) / ((Z_(U_s)(x) + Z_(U_s)(c_(b,k))) κ_(b,k))`: `w_m = Z_(V_m)` factors through `Z_(U_s)`. It has
degree `N − 2^s < N`. The verifier needs one `Z_(U_s)(t)` per block size, a short linearized recursion, then one
division per pair (as built, normalised and summed by groups: `PI2.md` §2).

The checks (as built: `PI2.md` §1, §2):

- `h(x) = (1 + 1_S(x))(1/(α + w(x) + βx) + 1/(α + P(x) + κ(x) + βσ(x)))` on `V_m`: the copy argument over the
  positions but the holes (the ports' own positions, `1_S` their indicator), `P` without `κ` and the ports, committed;
- the identity `h·d_pull·d_push + (1 + 1_S)(d_pull + d_push) + T = q·w_m(x)`, `T` the ports' term (§2.4.6), and `P`'s
  definition `P = P_v + w_m q_P` (`P_v` the pushes' formula above);
- the sum `Σ_{V_m} h = 0`.

**The multiset step.** The pulls' tags are the positions, the pushes' are `σ`'s values, both distinct: both sides are
sets of (value, tag) pairs, and mod-2 equality of sets is equality. So every position's value is the push meant for
it, and each value travels from its producing expression slot through forwards to exactly one consumer whose own push
is an expression (its *end*).

**The layout's invariant.** A push is a formula of its *row*, not of its own pull, so a pull constrains nothing unless
a formula of its row reads it, or it is forwarded: every pull that is an end is an input of its row's formula (the only
exception: a constant's segment ending in a spare slot). The Keccak rows of §2.4.1 satisfy it (`A_{j-1}` and `D_j` are
read by the χ formula, `P_{y'-1}` by the partial sum, `C[x-1][z]` by the D unit); the compiler checks it for every row
type (`Builder::finish`, §10.4 item 1).

**Booleanity.** A free bit is a self-loop pushing `b²` to itself, so `b = b²`; every other value is a degree-2 function
of bits and limbs, so every Keccak value is a bit.

**What it costs.** The indicators have degree `< N` and `E` degree `≤ 2N`, so `deg P_v < 3N`. Committing `P`
(`P_v`'s interpolation, below `N`) keeps the identity below `3N` and `q`, `q_P` below `2N`. (An unreduced product of a
region selector by a role selector would add `N`: the indicators must be the (block, role) cosets'.) The quotient table
is `h` (`3N` words), `q` in 2 chunks (`6N`), `P` (`N`, in `K`) and `q_P` (`2N`, in `K`): **`12N` words**, in a
sixteen-slot stack (`PI2.md` §2). No position is spent on outputs, and no gate quotient is needed.

### 2.4 D16v: the layout

Positions `i = 16y + k`, with role `k < 16` and row `y`. A row is the coset `ω_{16y} + V_4`. From position `x` of role
`k`, the row's cells are `w(x + ω_{k⊕c})` for `c < 16`. So at `t` the verifier needs the 16 values
`v_c = w(t + ω_c)`, and every role's formula reads them permuted by `XOR k`.

#### 2.4.1 Keccak-f in 130,944 positions

A round `j` (θ, ρπ, χ, ι) takes its input state `A_{j-1}`:

- `C_j[x][z] = Σ_y A_{j-1}[x,y][z]`;
- `D_j[x][z] = C_j[x-1][z] + C_j[x+1][z-1]`;
- `b_j[π(x,y)][z + r(x,y)] = A_{j-1}[x,y][z] + D_j[x][z]`;
- `A_j[x',y'][z'] = b + (1 + b_{x'+1}) b_{x'+2} + RC_j[z'][x' = y' = 0]`.

| row type (region) | rows a round | pulls (role: what) | pushes |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **`k0`**, plane `y' = 0` | 64 | 0–4: `A_{j-1}` of the 5 source lanes `π⁻¹(x', 0)` at `z' - r`; 5–9: their `D_j`; 10–15: three D units | 0–4: `A_j[x', 0]` (to round `j+1`); 5–9: `P_0[x'] = A_j[x', 0]` (to plane 1); D units: `D_{j+1}`, and a forward of `C` |
| **`k1`**, planes `y' = 1..4` | 256 | 0–4: `A_{j-1}`; 5–9: `D_j`; 10–14: `P_{y'-1}`; 15: a spare | 0–4: `A_j[x', y']`; 5–9: forwards (`D`'s chain); 10–14: `P_{y'} = P_{y'-1} + A_j[x', y']`, whose `P_4 = C_{j+1}[x'][z']` |
| **`kd`**, D units | | 8 units: `C[x-1][z]` (end), `C[x+1][z-1]` (forward) | `D[x][z]` (expression), `C`'s forward |
| **`kprep`**, round 0's `C_0` | | units of input bits | `C_0[x][z]`, forwards |

As built (§10.4 item 5), a permutation is 1,536 `k0` rows, 6,144 `k1`, 160 `kprep` and 344 `kd`: 8,184 rows,
**130,944 positions** (the design's count was 130,736), against the bit gate's 452,456, a 3.5× saving. ι is
`κ = RC_j[z]` at `k0`'s roles 0 and 5 (§10.4 item 4). The chains balance: `A_{j-1}` ends in its `k0`/`k1` pull (the
partial sums use `A_j` where it is produced); each `D[x][z]` feeds the 5 lanes `(x, y)`, whose planes `2x + 3y` are
distinct, so it ends in its one plane-0 consumer and is forwarded through the other four; `C` ends in one D unit and
is forwarded through the other. Degree 2 throughout.

**The last round makes only the lanes read.** A caller reads lanes 0 … 3 (the digest) at the end of a Keccak-256, the
whole state after a later block. Round 23's χ rows of plane `y` make lanes `5y … 5y + 4`, so a permutation that ends a
call needs only plane 0's 64 `k0` rows: `Permutation::permute` takes the lanes read; `Sponge::keccak256_lanes` asks for
25 in a later block and the caller's in the last (4, or `4 + 3k` for a wide draw's challenge `k`: planes 0 … 2 or
0 … 3). The planes not made leave their outputs `UNMADE`; their inputs (the lanes of `A_22` they would read) end at
free ends, and `D_23[x][z]` still ends at its `k0` row. Rounds 0 … 22 are whole (`C_23`, so `D_23`, reads every lane of
`A_22`), and the round-23 rows made are a whole permutation's, so every lane made is Keccak-f's. A digest's permutation
is 126,848 positions; at 128 signatures π2 has 60 (57 of π1's calls, the three claims') and three wide draws (2, 1, 2
planes fewer): 245 planes, −250,880 positions. The witness plan (`circuit::plan`) has four templates (a whole
permutation; last rounds of 1, 3 and 4 planes).

#### 2.4.2 Keccak-256 calls

- **One fresh sponge a call.** The first block is `A_{-1}` = the 32-byte state, the data and the padding (constants
  `0x01` and `0x80`), zeros in the capacity lanes. **Later blocks** are XOR units into the rate lanes: 2 pulls a bit.
- **The digest** is `A_23`'s lanes 0–3, forwarded into the next call's state bytes and packed into the challenge
  (`transcript::challenge_of`: the low 192 bits, limb 0 the lowest; a narrow challenge packs limb 2 from 32 bits). A
  wide draw's challenge `k` is three more lanes of the same output (lanes `1 + 3k … 3 + 3k`), packed as `Low`'s.
- **The seed**, `keccak(name ‖ IV ‖ public input)`, is one call on constants and the public input's bits.
- **π1-rec4 at the reference shape: 60 calls, 106 permutations, 46 later blocks**; the three claims' hashes add 17
  permutations (6, 6 and 5, at 27, 26 and 23 variables).

#### 2.4.3 Packing, bytes ↔ `E`

A scalar is 24 bytes, big-endian `c2 ‖ c1 ‖ c0`; a limb is `Σ_e bit_e x^e` in `K`'s polynomial basis. Packings are
Horner by 15-bit windows from the top (`pack`, and `pack-top4` for a 64-bit limb's top 4 bits; §10.4 item 9), one for
every read element and every challenge. The stream's hashed bits (and the public input's 256) are free cells: a
self-loop pushing `b²` and a slot that produces `b`, 2 positions a bit.

#### 2.4.4 `E` arithmetic on `K`-limbs

`E = K[y]/(y³ + y + 1)`. A product's limbs are degree-2 expressions in its operands' limbs:
`c₀ = a₀b₀ + a₁b₂ + a₂b₁`; `c₁ = a₀b₁ + a₁b₀ + a₁b₂ + a₂b₁ + a₂b₂`; `c₂ = a₀b₂ + a₁b₁ + a₂b₀ + a₂b₂`.

| what | how |
|---|---|
| products, squares and sums | units of 6 pulls, 3 expression pushes and 3 forwards; 2 units a row (§10.4 item 8) |
| constants | positions whose push is `κ`, in the Keccak rows' spare roles (§10.4 item 6) |
| bit fields of the row counts | the counts' bits are free cells; a wider field is a packing |
| zero checks | pushed to an outside tag with the value 0 |
| the ring kernel | expanded (§1.1) |
| spare slots | an expression slot with nothing to end pulls a constant's segment |

#### 2.4.5 Totals (measured, `tests/keccak_vm_rec.rs`, `tests/circuit.rs`)

At 128 signatures: 123 Keccak-f (π1-rec4's 106, the claims' 17; 63 with a trimmed last round) and about 0.56M other
positions (free bits, packings, later blocks' XORs, `E` units, ports): **16,413,312 of `2^24` (97.8%)**. At 400
signatures (108 + 17 Keccak-f): 16,684,592 (99.4%). With π1-rec3's 116 Keccak-f the three claims' hashes would need
17,972,736 positions, 1.2M over `2^24`: π1-rec4's 10 fewer permutations and the trimmed last rounds make the room.
π1-rec3's circuit at 128 signatures (the Lean's π1) hashes `C1`'s claim alone (96.5%, `tests/circuit.rs`,
`keccak_rec3_128_d16v`).

π1's main line (`KECCAK_MAIN`) at 32 signatures takes `N = 2^25` (53.6% used); a `2^25` circuit's `Q` would be
`2^28.58` words. (`pi2::synth::target_d16v`, the synthetic target of the PIOP's tests, was sized from the design-era
model.)

#### 2.4.6 Public inputs

The public inputs (`compile::HASHED_CLAIMS = [0, 1, 2]`) are the 32-byte public input and the Keccak-256 of each of
π1's three claims, which the contract reads from payloads 0, 1, 2 of the frame of PCS claims (`Read::Frame`) into π2's
seed. The row counts are not public: they are stream bytes hashed by π1's transcript (§10.4 item 3).

**A claim hashed in the circuit** (`Source::ClaimHash(j)`): its encoding as message bits, its Keccak-256
(`Sponge::keccak256`), the digest's four big-endian words a four-limb port. `C1`'s and `C2`'s roots and answers are the
stream's bits (π1's transcript hashed them; `units::Copies` gives an endable copy where the first block ends them),
`F`'s are constants; the coordinates, the values and the plain round's challenge are free bits (`compile::Given::Bit`,
replayed by the witness plan from the element's limbs) packed into limbs checked equal to the element's (an `E` sum, a
zero port).

**The fallback** (`compile_keeping`, `FIT_LOG_N = 24`): a circuit that does not fit `2^24` with the three hashes drops
the last and compiles again (`[0, 1]`, `[0]`, `[]`). The recursion's capacity (π1's `C1` at `2^28` words near 470
signatures) is kept, the bytes are not: the contract then encodes the claim, its root `C2` (32 bytes, as it is) and
distinct elements (the out-of-domain answer, the narrow challenges as their low 20 bytes, `Read::Narrow`, the values)
in π2's stream, the elements ports. No vector exercises it at 128 or 400 signatures.

**Each public word `J` binds through one port**, a hole of its producing row whose formula is the fingerprint of the
word's limbs, which must be `F_J`. The hole pushes it to an outside tag `τ_J ∉ V_m`; π2's PIOP (`PI2.md` §1) checks it
apart from the copy argument, by the identity's term `T = Σ_port I_p (E_p + Φ_k)`, `Φ_k(x) = Σ_J F_J w_m(x)/(w_r(x) +
ω_{y_J})` (`F_J` on the hole's row, zero on the others): a fraction a port for the verifier. A three-limb port's
fingerprint (an element, a zero check) is the element `L₀ + YL₁ + Y²L₂`; a four-limb port's (the statement, a root, a
claim's hash) is `(L₀ + YL₁) + γ(L₂ + YL₃)` (`PI2.md` §4 item 2). π1's zero checks are ports of value 0, in no `Φ`:
they cost the verifier nothing. A pair's two coordinates are not ports: the port is its challenge `z`, and the calls'
words are `y_0(z)`, `y_1(z)` (`CallWord::Pair`, §12). `crates/membership/quotient`'s alternative, the public weight
inside `h`'s identity, would raise `q`'s degree; `T` is below `2N`, inside the identity's `3N`.

## 4. π2's protocol

The protocol as built is `PI2.md` §2. In outline:

- **Setup** (the contract's constants): the setup's commitment (`κ`, `κ + σ`: root and out-of-domain answer); π2's
  IV, `keccak("leanvm-rec-iv-v3" ‖ π1's contract IV ‖ the setup root ‖ its answer ‖ the circuit's digest ‖ log_n)`
  (`prover::iv`); π1's setup commitment `F`.
- **Public inputs:** the public input (32 B) and π1's three claims' Keccak-256s (frame payloads 0 … 2); with the
  fallback (§2.4.6), the public data of the claims the contract encodes, in the stream.
- **The transcript** (Keccak-256, `KeccakChannel`): the seed `keccak("leanvm-rec-pi2v3" ‖ IV ‖ public input ‖ the
  claims' hashes)`; `W`'s commitment (root, answer), then `α`, `β`, `γ`; `Q`'s commitment, then `t`; the row's 16 fold
  values `a_k`, `ξ = κ(t) + βσ(t)` and `s = P(t)` (18 values); then `ρ`.
- **The verifier** at `t`: the digits; the row's cells from the `a_k`; the (block, role) indicators; `P_v(t)`; the
  ports' `Φ` and their term; `d_pull`, `d_push = α + s + ξ`; the identity and `P`'s definition, as fractions with one
  inversion at the end.
- **Claims** (5, each a point of its commitment's whole stack): `W` at `(ρ, ρ², ρ⁴, ρ⁸, w_4(t), …, w_23(t))`; the setup
  at `(w_0(t), …, w_23(t), β)`, value `ξ`; on `Q`'s sixteen slots, the identity's combination of `h(t)` and `q(t)` (at
  `(u_0, …, u_24, ρ', y_0, y_1)`), `P(t) = s` and `P`'s definition combined by `ρ` (at `(u_0, …, u_24, ρ, 1, 1)`), and
  `Σ_V h = 0` at the top coefficient's point.
- **The contract**: the claims' hashes from the frame into the seed; the stream; π2's verifier; π2's 5 claims declared
  at payloads 3 … 7 (`W`, the setup, `Q`'s three).

## 5. Soundness (modelled; the Lean bounds)

Each challenge's error is its Schwartz–Zippel bound times the lists of the commitments it is drawn against (the
tables a root alone admits: WHIR takes no out-of-domain sample at level 0). The setup is honest, so `L = 1`.
`params/leanvm_rec_params.py` prints these rounds; an adversary of `Q` permutation queries breaks a layer with
probability about `Q (ε_WHIR + ε_round)`, the openings charged once at `269 · 2^-137 = 2^-128.93` a query and
`ε_round` the weakest round after the lists (`docs/leanvm/univariate/SPEC.md` §9).

**π2's rounds**, at `N = 2^24` over the stacks of §2.5 (bits alone, after the lists):

| round | error | alone | after |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| the copy argument `(α, β, γ)`: `2N` fractions, the ports' poles (`PI2.md` §7), the four four-limb ports (the statement, the three claims' hashes; degree 1 in `γ`), over `W`'s list 9,952 | `(4N + 4)/2^192` | 166.0 | 152.7 |
| `t`: the identity and `P`'s definition, degree `< 4N` with `Q`'s prover-filled empty slots (`< 3N` for the honest `Q`; `PI2.md` §2), over `W`'s and `Q`'s lists (9,952 · 3,564) | `4N/2^192` | 166.0 | **140.9** |
| `ρ`: `W`'s fold (degree 15), over both lists | `15/2^192` | 188.1 | 163.0 |
| `ρ`: `Q`'s claim (degree 3), over both lists | `3/2^192` | 190.4 | 165.3 |

With its openings once, π2 keeps **128.93 bits** as `Q/ε` at `Q = 2^64`. The Lean (`formal/RecPiop`'s `err`) states
coarser rounds, `(4N − 1 + Σ_j d_j)/|E|`, `(4N − 1)/|E|`, `2^r/|E|`, for `f96a7938`'s π2, and proves the same bound
(`RecCompose.pi2Bound`, `pi2Bound_le_bits`).

**π1-rec4's rounds** are the main line's (`docs/leanvm/univariate/SPEC.md` §9; `C1`'s list 4,473, `C2`'s 6,296 at 128
signatures) but for these (bits after the lists, `python3 -I params/leanvm_univariate_params.py`):

| query | error | π1-rec3 | π1-rec4 |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `α`'s call | the bus `16 · side`, `γ` 60, the eq point `2^22 − 1` (§1.4); one query in π1-rec4, the errors summed | 154.3, 174.0, 157.9 | 154.1 |
| `t`'s call | the identities' `Σ deg` (+ the ring map's 6) | 143.2 | 143.2 |
| `ρ`'s call | `ρ` 115 (+ `λ` 2 + the ring map's 6) | 160.4 | 160.3 |
| the batch's pairs | degree 8, a narrow challenge: `8/2^160` | **132.3** | **132.3** |

The weakest round is the batch's narrow pairs; with the three openings once, **128.79 bits as `Q/ε` at `Q = 2^64`**
(128.86 at 400 signatures). The Lean's cap for π1-rec3 (every query ≤ `2^35/|E|`, lists `9,952²`: 128.49 bits,
`UniKeccakSound.uniKnowledgeSoundnessKeccakRec3_closed`) holds for π1-rec4's summed queries too.

**π2's circuit.** (a) The trimmed last round (§2.4.1) constrains every lane the caller reads as a whole permutation
does: plane `y`'s χ formula reads only the lanes of `A_22` that `π` sends to it and `D_23`, which rounds 0 … 22 and the D
units constrain; the unmade planes are never read (`Sponge` asserts it). (b) A wide challenge's packing reads three
output lanes of one permutation, as `Low` reads three of the digest. (c) A satisfied circuit carries the Keccak-256 of
each of π1's claims as the graph computes it, at Keccak-f[1600]; the contract binds each hash to its payload, and a
payload binds its claim by Keccak-256's collision resistance, which the frame of PCS claims assumes for every declared
claim (the EIP's assumption).

**The composition.** π2's knowledge soundness gives a π1 transcript on which π1's verifier (its graph, Keccak-256 bit
for bit) outputs the claims whose hashes the frame declares; the block proves them, and π1's soundness applies. The
errors add: π2's, π1-rec4's and the merge hazard's (`2^16/|E|` a query, `RecGraphKeccak.hazard_probK`): **127.86 bits**
as `Q/ε` at `Q = 2^64` (127.70 with the Lean's cap on π1's rounds). It is below 128 because WHIR's `269 · 2^-137` a query
is charged twice, once in π2's bound (which covers all eight openings) and again inside π1's; charged once, the sum
would be 128.79 bits. That needs a tighter composition proof, no code change; the 127.86 bits are accepted as they are.
The Lean statement of this composition is `formal/RecCompose`, for `f96a7938`'s π2 over π1-rec3 (π1-rec3's two terms at
the real Keccak-f[1600]); what it needs for π1-rec4 and the hashed claims: `docs/LEAN_PORT.md`.

**Fiat–Shamir.** Both layers are random-oracle Fiat–Shamir over Keccak-256. A cheating prover can grind π1's
challenges offline, as it could on chain: the per-query bounds above hold, with `Q` queries.

### 10.4 Decisions and deviations from §2.3–§2.6

1. **The balancing is automatic.** Each value is placed at its readers: forwards, and at most one expression slot (its
   end). A value with no slot placement ends at a **free end** (a slot whose pull nothing places and no formula of its
   row reads); a slot whose push nothing asks for but whose pull is placed pushes **junk**. Every non-port slot
   produces one value and every placed slot ends one, so the values without an end and the free ends are equally many,
   and any matching closes every chain (the last call's unused lanes, round 23's `C_24`, bits read only by forwards). A
   slot with neither is **dead**: a self-loop of 0, its formula vanishing on its row. `Builder::finish` checks: a value
   is placed at most at one slot, a free value at none; every cell a live formula reads is placed; every placed cell is
   read by some formula of its row.
2. **The public side has holes.** A push to an outside tag leaves its position's tag unpushed: `σ` has as many holes as
   ports, the ports' own positions (the last role of a port row; the port rows are blocks, so the holes are cosets).
   The PIOP leaves the holes out of the pulls (`PI2.md` §4 item 1): a hole's value is unconstrained, harmless because
   no formula reads a hole (`pi2::circuit::Circuit::validate`, asserted by `Spec::of` and `Setup::new`).
3. **The row counts are not public.** They are stream bytes hashed by π1's transcript, and nothing on chain reads them.
   The ports are the statement and the claims' hashes (4 limbs each), for a claim the contract encodes its root (4
   limbs) and distinct elements (3 limbs), and the zero checks (3 limbs); the calls' constant words stay constants.
4. **ι by `κ`:** `κ` is allowed at any expression slot, and the formulas have no constant term. D16v: `κ = RC_j[z]` at
   `k0` roles 0 and 5 (no split of plane 0 by `RC`). D32v: at the 7 roles whose push holds `A[0,0]`.
5. **D16v's Keccak-f is 130,944 positions** (the design: 130,736): `kprep` is 160 rows (two `C_0` units of 5 forwards
   and a sum, and two D units; the input ends at round 0's χ slot, so its `kprep` reads are forwards); `kd` is 344 rows,
   the D units drawn from a pool (`k0`'s, `kprep`'s, then `kd`'s); every round's rows are made first.
6. **Constants live in the Keccak rows' spare roles**: D16v's `k1` role 15; D32v's roles 30–31 and the last round's
   25–31. A spare is an expression slot pushing 0, dead when unused: no constants region.
7. **D32v's rounds:** `prep` (θ of the input), `prep-first` for a first block (its capacity lanes not read), 23
   `round`s (χ, ι, the next θ), and `last` (χ and ι only).
8. **`E` units:** the operand that ends at the unit is the one with fewer uses left; a unit where neither can end
   forwards both and has three more slots (9 positions instead of 6); squares are products with `b = a`; the limbs of a
   statement word, integer or bit field are fresh zero constants beyond the low one.
9. **Packings** are Horner by 15-bit windows from the top: `pack` is `acc·x^15 + Σ b_i x^i`, `pack-top4` a 64-bit
   limb's top 4 bits. Absent bits read a shared zero; a wider top window starts from a fresh zero.
10. **Free bits:** every bit is a self-loop with a copy (one region). A bit at a first block's slot uses the copy; the
    others leave it junk.
11. **Reads never hashed** (the batch's 3 end values, 9 limbs) are **free values**: cycles of forwards, any word,
    bound by the ports, or for a hashed claim by its encoding's bits.
12. **Blocks are padded** (`layout::pad_counts`). A region of `c` rows padded to `k` binary digits is `k` aligned blocks,
    so fewer digits mean fewer selector pairs. Each region starts at one digit; while the padded total exceeds the power
    of two the unpadded rows need, the region whose next step saves the most rows per added digit (per expression role)
    takes it. The step **looks past digit counts that save nothing**: `keccak16/k1` for π1-rec3 at 400 signatures
    (724,992 rows) pads to 786,432 at both 2 and 3 digits and saves 61K rows from 3 to 4, so a one-digit greedy gave 70
    blocks and 429 selector pairs where the look-ahead gives 22 and 144. Port regions are not padded. A fuller circuit
    has less room to pad: 176 pairs at 97.8%.
13. **The witness generator evaluates the circuit's own formulas** from the free inputs (bits, never-hashed limbs);
    `eval::evaluate` is the reference it is checked against. Per proof the witness is replayed from `circuit::plan`
    (about 0.01 s).
