# Recursion: an EVM verifier for the Keccak VM

A proof of a Keccak VM execution (leanVM `9d50c92a3`'s tables, `docs/leanvm/univariate/KECCAK.md`) verified on the EVM
by recursion: π1 is the univariate proof, π2 a SNARK of π1's verifier, and the contract runs π2's verifier only. At the
reference shape (128 signatures) the real proof verifies for **28,459 gas of execution, 43,819 under EIP-7623** (1,005 B
of calldata, 7 claims declared as 6; 27,641 and 42,997 with `PCS_OPEN`; π2v2, `PI2.md`), apart from the frame of
claims (193 B) and their 11,000 intrinsic gas. Code: `crates/leanvm/rec`; π2's PIOP in
[`PI2.md`](PI2.md); the claims' points in [`SPEC-POINTS.md`](SPEC-POINTS.md).

Every number is marked:

- **measured**: by `crates/leanvm/rec`'s tests (§10, §11), on the Keccak VM's committed real proofs or on the recorded
  verifier at a shape;
- **modelled**: computed by `params/leanvm_rec_params.py` (`python3 -I`), from the measured counts: the soundness
  figures (§5), the other arithmetizations' sizes (§3), the batch's rounds of `k` variables (`SPEC-POINTS.md` §2.4).

```sh
export RUSTFLAGS="-C target-cpu=native"
cargo test --release -p leanvm_rec -- --nocapture                                            # circuit, PIOP, contract
cargo test --release -p leanvm_rec --test keccak_vm_rec -- --ignored --nocapture             # π1-rec3 under π2 at 128 and 400 (§1.2, §10.3)
cargo test --release -p leanvm_rec --test counts -- --include-ignored --nocapture            # π1's graph, its counts (§1)
cargo test --release -p leanvm_rec --test pi2_real -- --ignored --nocapture real_end_to_end  # the real proof end to end (§11)
```

## 0. Summary

- **π1** is the univariate PIOP of `crates/leanvm/univariate` on the Keccak VM, with the Fiat–Shamir shape
  **π1-rec3** (`leanvm_univariate::KECCAK_REC3 = Variant { skip: 3, fs: FsShape::Rec3 }`):
  - the bus's `α` and Flock's zerocheck eq point are successive squares of one challenge each (§1.4);
  - the batching sumcheck runs two variables a round, with narrow (160-bit) challenges (§12, `SPEC-POINTS.md`).

  Its statement and commitments are the main line's (`KECCAK_MAIN`); its proof differs in the derived challenges and
  the batch's rounds. Its claims are 3 point claims (`C1`, `C2`, setup `F`) at one point generated by 14 narrow
  challenges.
- **π2** is a univariate quotient SNARK, in the family of `crates/membership/quotient`. It proves that π1's verifier,
  without its PCS openings, accepts on the public data and outputs these PCS claims. Its arithmetization is
  **D16v** (§2.4):
  - one copy argument, in which a position's **push is a degree-2 expression of its row** (a "virtual push"), so
    nothing but the wiring is checked;
  - rows of 16 positions;
  - Keccak-f in 130,944 positions;
  - `E` in `K`-limbs.
- **The contract** verifies π2 and checks π1's three claims and π2's four declared (point claims,
  `machine.rs`'s encoding).

| measured, `c = 0`, no 21,000 base | 128 signatures (reference) | 400 signatures |
|---|---:|---:|
| π1's verifier: Keccak-256 calls / Keccak-f / stream | 70 / 116 / 9,232 B | 71 / 118 / 9,424 B |
| π2's positions (D16v, `N = 2^24`) | 15,668,416 (93.4%) | 15,940,800 (95.0%) |
| calldata (zero bytes) | 1,004 B (59) | 1,004 B (61) |
| execution | **27,641** | 28,421 |
| **EIP-7623** (its floor) | **42,997** (38,390) | 43,753 (38,330) |
| EIP-7976 (64 a byte) | 64,256 | 64,256 |
| claims / calls | 7 / 6 | 7 / 6 |

The univariate verifier on chain, without recursion, is about 359K at 128 signatures
(`docs/leanvm/univariate/KECCAK.md`). π1's main line does not fit π2: at 32 signatures it already needs `N = 2^25`
(§2.4.5), whose quotient table exceeds WHIR's `2^28` words. That is why the recursion proves π1-rec3.

## 1. π1's verifier, measured

### 1.1 Method

The univariate verifier, run on the recorder's symbol (`leanvm_statement::evm::record`), is a hash-consed graph. Its
nodes are:

- the stream's reads;
- the Keccak-256 chain (`Hash(state, from, to)`: one Keccak-256 of the 32-byte state and the stream's bytes
  `from..to`; `Low`: its low 192 bits; `Narrow`: its low 160 bits);
- `E`'s `Add` and `Mul`, and constants;
- bit fields of the row counts;
- the ring switch's kernel;
- the checks;
- the claims' words.

This graph is the circuit π2 proves. In `crates/leanvm/rec`:

- **`src/count.rs`** counts the **live** nodes: those the calls or the checks read, through their operands. The ring
  kernel is expanded by recording `ring_tower::weight_at` on fresh symbols.
- **`src/eval.rs`** evaluates every node on a real proof. `tests/counts.rs`,
  `the_graph_is_the_verifier_on_real_proofs`: on the three committed Keccak VM vectors (`keccak.txt`, the main line
  at 32 signatures; `keccak128_rec3.txt` and `keccak_rec3.txt`, π1-rec3 at 128 and 400), the evaluated graph writes
  exactly the vectors' claims, every check holds, and a flipped stream byte breaks them. **Measured.** This
  is the reference π2's witness is checked against.

### 1.2 The counts (measured, `Claims::Point`, π1-rec3)

| | 128 signatures (`REFERENCE`) | 400 signatures |
|---|---:|---:|
| stream bytes | 9,232 | 9,424 |
| Keccak-256 calls (seed included) | 70 | 71 |
| Keccak-f[1600] permutations | 116 | 118 |
| `E` products / linear operations (kernels expanded) | 8,476 / 6,633 | 8,673 / 6,887 |
| claims: points / distinct challenges / values | 3 / 14 / 3 | 3 / 14 / 3 |

`tests/keccak_vm_rec.rs` records these on a sample stream (`keccak_vm_under_pi2`); `tests/counts.rs`'s `count_report`
prints the full table (reads, constants, checks, packings) for `KECCAK_MAIN` and `KECCAK_REC3` at the reference shape.

- **The verifier is linearised.** It inverts nothing; its checks are against zero.
- **Flock's terminal is the structured one** (`docs/leanvm/flock/KECCAK.md`, M3): about 3K `E` operations at a skip
  of 3, which is why the arithmetic is small next to the Keccak-f.

### 1.3 The transcript

π1-rec3's challenges, in order (`tests/counts.rs`, `transcript_shape`, prints the run-length list of each hash's bytes):

1. the seed: name, the contract's IV, the public input (80 B, no state);
2. the six row counts and `C1`'s root, then `α = (c, c², c⁴, c⁸)` (one hash), `β`, `γ`;
3. Flock: the zerocheck's eq point (its fixed coordinates, then one challenge and its squares), the skip's coset
   values, the zerocheck's and the lincheck's rounds, the slices and the ring map's `f_0..f_5`;
4. `C2`'s root, then `t`; the values at `t`, then `ρ`; `λ`;
5. the batching sumcheck: one narrow challenge a pair of variables (at 27 variables 13 pairs and one plain round).

Where the permutations go:

- **One challenge costs at least one permutation.** A plain sumcheck round (48 B and the state) fits one block; a
  pair's 8 scalars and the state are 224 B, two blocks.
- **Data costs one more permutation per 136 bytes** (the slices, the values at `t`).
- **A permutation is a fresh call or a later block**: 116 − 70 = 46 later blocks at the reference shape.
- **Draws with no new bytes are the waste**, which π1-rec3 removes where soundness allows (§1.4).

### 1.4 The derived challenges

**π1-rec3 derives `α_2, α_3, α_4` from `α_1`, and Flock's zerocheck coordinates after its fixed ones from the first,
each the square of the one before** (`FsShape::draw`; `flock_min::zerocheck`; the prover's
`flock::zerocheck::skip::EqPoint::Squares`, and `α` squared in `E` as the verifier does).

- **The zerocheck's eq point.** `Σ_x eq(r, x) P(x)` is multilinear in each `r_i`. So the substitution
  `r_i = c^(2^i)` maps distinct monomials to distinct powers of `c` (a base-2 Kronecker substitution), and the
  polynomial stays nonzero when `P ≠ 0` on the cube. Over `n` derived coordinates its degree in `c` is `2^n − 1`: the
  error goes from `n/|E|` to `2^n/|E|`.
- **The bus's `α`.** Each fingerprint `Σ_s eq(α, s) τ_s` is multilinear in `α`, so with `α = (c, c², c⁴, c⁸)` it is a
  polynomial `f_τ(c)` of degree ≤ 15. Distinct tuples give distinct `f_τ`, since the 16 eq polynomials at the power
  point are a basis of the polynomials of degree < 16. `β` stays an independent challenge. Then `Π(β + f_τ(c))` as
  polynomials in `β` over `E[c]` are equal only if the multisets `{f_τ}` are, because the factors are irreducible in
  `β`. Schwartz–Zippel over `(β, c)` at degree 16 a factor.
- **Not derived:**
  - **`β` from `α`.** Products of the monic degree-16 polynomials `c^16 + f_τ(c)` can coincide for different
    multisets.
  - **The ring map's `f_0..f_5`.** The tower argument needs an independent `f_j` a level.
  - **`γ`.** One permutation; it could be `β²` by a union bound, which saves one.
- **A sponge squeeze (SHAKE-like, 5 challenges a permutation) is not taken.** The constraint is Keccak-256.
- **Larger skips do not help**: the skip's coset values cost blocks.
- **Merging sumchecks does not help.** Their rounds are already one permutation each, and the lincheck cannot join the
  batch.

### 1.5 The claims, and what the contract must carry

The three claims, on `C1`, `C2` and the setup `F`, are at the batching sumcheck's point `r`: each at `r`'s prefix of
its commitment's variables (`verifier::batch`). `r` has the largest stack's variables: 27 at the reference shape, 28
at 400 signatures.

The Keccak VM never commits Flock's matrices `A0`, `B0`, so there are no claims on them. The contract needs `r` and
the 3 values. `r` is generated by 14 narrow challenges `z_j` (`SPEC-POINTS.md`), from which the contract derives the
coordinates: **17 elements, 408 B**. They are π1's challenges, computed inside π2 and never on chain, so they must be
in calldata.

They cannot be made fewer (`SPEC-POINTS.md` §2):

- **Sumcheck challenges are sequential.** A point derived from one seed would let the prover know them in advance.
- **The batching sumcheck turns π1's weighted relations into one point claim per commitment**, and `q_flock`'s ring
  switch needs a fresh sumcheck over its variables; DEEP, Gemini, Zeromorph and a univariate batch give the
  coordinates back as values.

## 2. π2's arithmetization

### 2.1 The reference: `crates/membership/quotient` (measured)

- **One gate, `V = X + C + Y·C`, four cells a row.** It is a χ on bits, an XOR when `Y = 0`, and a NOT.
- **The wiring:** one logarithmic-derivative copy argument, with unique tags on both sides. It is sound in
  characteristic 2, since both sides are sets.
- **One quotient identity at `t`.** The verifier is sent 5 values.

| | measured |
|---|---|
| rows per Keccak-f (one Keccak-256 of a 64-byte block) | **113,114** |
| the Merkle path: 34 permutations | 3,878,612 of `2^22` rows (92.5%); at most 37 permutations fit |
| words per row at `2^22` rows | witness 5.00 (hidden), wiring 4.00, quotient **63.00** (`2^27.98`, hidden) |
| words per cell (4 a row) | quotient 15.75 hidden; 13.75 without hiding (`h` 3, `q` 9, gate 1.75) |

π2 needs no zero knowledge. π1 has none, and π2's witness is π1's proof, which is not secret.

### 2.2 Why bit-level wiring does not fit

With `crates/membership/quotient`'s gate, π1-rec3's 116 permutations alone need 116 × 113,114 = 13.1M rows
(`2^23.65`): a `2^29.6`-word quotient table, 3.1× over WHIR's `2^28`.

The copy argument costs about 9 words a wired cell (`h` 3, `q` 6, both in `E`), whatever the gate. So what matters is
the number of wired cells a Keccak round needs:

- **The XOR-per-row circuit spends about 18,900 a round.** That is 113,114 rows a permutation, of 4 cells.
- **The floor with cell outputs is about 3,840 a round.** Every state bit must be written once and read once across
  ρπ's rotations, and θ adds its column parities.
- **Rotations cannot be algebraic in characteristic 2.** `z ↦ z + r mod 64` is not GF(2)-affine: no affine map of
  `GF(2)^6` has order 64. And there is no element of order 64 for a multiplicative "next" map. So ρ is always wiring.
- **The constraint on the other side is the verifier's calldata.** Every relation the verifier checks at `t` among
  `L` cells costs `L` values, or a sumcheck.

### 2.3 Virtual pushes

**The copy argument carries the computation.** Every position `i` of the witness `w` (a `K`-word, `N = 2^m`
positions on the Cantor subspace `V_m`) has:

- a **pull** `(w(ω_i), ω_i)`: its value, tagged with its own point;
- a **push** `(P(ω_i), σ(ω_i))`: a value `P(ω_i)` sent to the position `σ(ω_i)`, a fixed permutation of the
  positions (setup), or to a tag outside `V_m` (§2.4.6).

`P(ω_i)` is one of:

- a **forward**: its own pull, `P = w`;
- an **expression slot**: a polynomial `E_{b,k}` of degree ≤ 2 in the 16 values of its row, chosen by the position's
  **role** `k` (its low 4 index bits) and its row's **region block** `b` (an aligned block of rows);
- a **constant**: `κ(ω_i)`, a setup column that is zero off the constant positions.

So `P = w + Σ_(b,k) I_(b,k)·(E_(b,k) − w) + κ`, where `I_(b,k)` is the indicator of the positions of role `k` in
block `b`.

That set is a coset of `U_s = span(β_4, …, β_(3+s))`, for a block of `2^s` rows. So
`I_(b,k)(x) = w_m(x) / ((Z_(U_s)(x) + Z_(U_s)(c_(b,k))) κ_(b,k))`: `w_m = Z_(V_m)` factors through `Z_(U_s)`. It has
degree `N − 2^s < N`. The verifier needs one `Z_(U_s)(t)` per block size, a short linearized recursion, and then one
division per pair (as built, normalised and summed by groups: `PI2.md` §2).

The checks are (as built, π2v2: `PI2.md` §1, §2):

- `h(x) = (1 + 1_S(x))(1/(α + w(x) + βx) + 1/(α + P(x) + κ(x) + βσ(x)))` on `V_m`: the copy argument over the
  positions but the holes (the ports' own positions, `1_S` their indicator), `P` without `κ` and the ports, committed;
- the identity `h·d_pull·d_push + (1 + 1_S)(d_pull + d_push) + T = q·w_m(x)`, with `w_m = Z_{V_m}` and `T` the
  ports' term (§2.4.6), and `P`'s definition `P = P_v + w_m q_P` (`P_v` the pushes' formula below);
- the sum `Σ_{V_m} h = 0`.

**Soundness of the multiset step.** The pulls' tags are the positions, all distinct. The pushes' tags are `σ`'s
values, also distinct. So both sides are sets of (value, tag) pairs, and mod-2 equality of sets is equality (as in
`crates/membership/quotient`). Hence every position's value is the push meant for it. Each value then travels from
its producing expression slot through forwards to exactly one consumer whose own push is an expression (its *end*).

**The layout's invariant.** A position's push is a formula of its *row*, not of its own pull, so a pull constrains
nothing unless some formula of its row reads it, or it is forwarded. So:

- every pull that is an end is an input of its row's formula;
- the only exception is a segment of a constant, ending in a spare slot, whose value is irrelevant.

The Keccak rows of §2.4.1 satisfy it:

- `A_{j-1}` and `D_j` are read by the χ formula;
- `P_{y'-1}` is read by the partial sum;
- `C[x-1][z]` is read by the D unit.

The layout compiler checks it for every row type (`Builder::finish`, §10.4 item 1).

**Booleanity.** A free bit is a self-loop pushing `b²` to itself, so `b = b²`. Every other value is a degree-2
function of bits and limbs, so by induction every Keccak value is a bit.

**What it costs.**

- **The degree.** The indicators have degree `N − 2^s < N` and `E` degree `≤ 2N`, so `deg P_v < 3N`. Committing `P`
  (`P_v`'s interpolation, below `N`) keeps the identity below `3N` and `q` below `2N`; `P`'s definition quotient `q_P`
  is below `2N` too. (π2v1 kept `P_v` inside `d_push`: the identity below `5N`, `q` below `4N`.) A product of a region
  selector by a role selector, unreduced, would add `N`: the indicators must be those of the (block, role) cosets.
- **The words.** The quotient table is `h` (`3N` words), `q` in 2 chunks (`6N`), `P` (`N`, in `K`) and `q_P` (`2N`, in
  `K`): **`12N` words** (π2v1: `h` and `q` in 4 chunks, `15N`).
- **What it saves.** No position is spent on outputs, and no gate quotient is needed: every constraint is a push.

### 2.4 D16v: the layout

Positions `i = 16y + k`, with role `k < 16` and row `y`. A row is the coset `ω_{16y} + V_4`. From position `x` of role
`k`, the row's cells are `w(x + ω_{k⊕c})` for `c < 16`. So at `t` the verifier needs the 16 values
`v_c = w(t + ω_c)`, and every role's formula reads them permuted by `XOR k`.

#### 2.4.1 Keccak-f in 130,944 positions

A round `j` (θ, ρπ, χ, ι) takes its input state `A_{j-1}`:

- `C_j[x][z] = Σ_y A_{j-1}[x,y][z]`;
- `D_j[x][z] = C_j[x-1][z] + C_j[x+1][z-1]`;
- `b_j[π(x,y)][z + r(x,y)] = A_{j-1}[x,y][z] + D_j[x][z]`;
- `A_j[x',y'][z'] = b + (1 + b_{x'+1}) b_{x'+2} + RC_j[z'][x' = y' = 0]`.

| row type (region) | rows a round | pulls (role: what) | pushes |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **`k0`**, plane `y' = 0` | 64 | 0–4: `A_{j-1}` of the 5 source lanes `π⁻¹(x', 0)` at `z' - r`; 5–9: their `D_j`; 10–15: three D units | 0–4: `A_j[x', 0]` (to round `j+1`); 5–9: `P_0[x'] = A_j[x', 0]` (to plane 1); D units: `D_{j+1}`, and a forward of `C` |
| **`k1`**, planes `y' = 1..4` | 256 | 0–4: `A_{j-1}`; 5–9: `D_j`; 10–14: `P_{y'-1}`; 15: a spare | 0–4: `A_j[x', y']`; 5–9: forwards (`D`'s chain); 10–14: `P_{y'} = P_{y'-1} + A_j[x', y']`, whose `P_4 = C_{j+1}[x'][z']` |
| **`kd`**, D units | | 8 units: `C[x-1][z]` (end), `C[x+1][z-1]` (forward) | `D[x][z]` (expression), `C`'s forward |
| **`kprep`**, round 0's `C_0` | | units of input bits | `C_0[x][z]`, forwards |

As built (§10.4 item 5), a permutation is 1,536 `k0` rows, 6,144 `k1`, 160 `kprep` and 344 `kd`: 8,184 rows,
**130,944 positions** (the design's count was 130,736), against `crates/membership/quotient`'s 452,456, a 3.5× saving.
ι is `κ = RC_j[z]` at `k0`'s roles 0 and 5 (§10.4 item 4). The chains balance:

- `A_{j-1}` ends in its `k0`/`k1` pull, fan-out 1: the partial sums use `A_j` where it is produced.
- `D` ends in its one plane-0 consumer. Each `D[x][z]` feeds the 5 lanes `(x, y)`, whose planes `2x + 3y` are
  distinct, so exactly one is plane 0. It is forwarded through the other four.
- `C` ends in one D unit and is forwarded through the other.

Degree 2 throughout.

#### 2.4.2 Keccak-256 calls

- **One fresh sponge a call.** The first block is `A_{-1}` = the 32-byte state, the data and the padding (constants
  `0x01` and `0x80`), with zeros in the capacity lanes.
- **Later blocks** are XOR units into the rate lanes: 2 pulls a bit.
- **The digest** is `A_23`'s lanes 0–3. It is forwarded into the next call's state bytes, and packed into the challenge
  (`transcript::challenge_of`: the low 192 bits, limb 0 the lowest; a narrow challenge packs limb 2 from 32 bits).
- **The seed**, `keccak(name ‖ IV ‖ public input)`, is one call on constants and the public input's bits.
- **π1-rec3 at the reference shape: 70 calls, 116 permutations, 46 later blocks.**

#### 2.4.3 Packing, bytes ↔ `E`

- A scalar is 24 bytes, big-endian `c2 ‖ c1 ‖ c0`. A limb is `Σ_e bit_e x^e` in `K`'s polynomial basis.
- Packings are Horner by 15-bit windows from the top (`pack`, and `pack-top4` for a 64-bit limb's top 4 bits; §10.4
  item 9). A packing is needed for every read element and every challenge.
- The stream's hashed bits (and the public input's 256) are free cells: a self-loop pushing `b²`, and a slot that
  produces `b`. That is 2 positions a bit.

#### 2.4.4 `E` arithmetic on `K`-limbs

`E = K[y]/(y³ + y + 1)`. A product's limbs are degree-2 expressions in its operands' limbs:

- `c₀ = a₀b₀ + a₁b₂ + a₂b₁`;
- `c₁ = a₀b₁ + a₁b₀ + a₁b₂ + a₂b₁ + a₂b₂`;
- `c₂ = a₀b₂ + a₁b₁ + a₂b₀ + a₂b₂`.

How the operations are laid out:

| what | how |
|---|---|
| products, squares and sums (8,476 and 6,633 at the reference shape) | units of 6 pulls, 3 expression pushes and 3 forwards; 2 units a row (§10.4 item 8) |
| constants | positions whose push is `κ`, the setup's constants column, in the Keccak rows' spare roles (§10.4 item 6) |
| bit fields of the row counts | the counts' bits are free cells; a wider field is a packing |
| zero checks | pushed to an outside tag with the value 0 |
| the ring kernel | expanded (§1.1) |
| spare slots | an expression slot with nothing to end pulls a constant's segment |

|---|---:|---:|

π1's main line (`KECCAK_MAIN`) at 32 signatures takes `N = 2^25` (53.6% used): it does not fit `2^24`, and a `2^25`
circuit's `Q` would be `2^28.91` words. So π1 must be π1-rec3. (`pi2::synth::target_d16v`, the synthetic target the
PIOP's tests use, was sized from the design-era model, not from the Keccak VM.)

#### 2.4.6 Public inputs

The public inputs are:

- the 32-byte public input;
- π1's two roots;
- π1's claims' distinct elements: 14 narrow challenges and 3 values.

The row counts are not public: they are stream bytes hashed by π1's transcript, and nothing on chain reads them
(§10.4 item 3).

**Each element `J` binds through one port**, a hole of its producing row whose formula is the fingerprint of the
element's limbs, which must be `F_J`. In the circuit the hole pushes it to an outside tag `τ_J ∉ V_m`; π2v2's PIOP
(`PI2.md` §1) checks it apart from the copy argument, by the identity's term `T = Σ_port I_p (E_p + Φ_k)`, `Φ_k(x) =
Σ_J F_J w_m(x)/(w_r(x) + ω_{y_J})` (`F_J` on the hole's row, zero on the others): the verifier's cost is a fraction a
port. A three-limb port's fingerprint (an element, a zero check) is the element itself, `L₀ + YL₁ + Y²L₂`; a
four-limb port's (the statement, a root) is `(L₀ + YL₁) + γ(L₂ + YL₃)` (`PI2.md` §4.2). π1's zero checks are ports of
value 0: in no `Φ`, they cost the verifier nothing (π2v1 summed their outside tags in closed form, §12 item 6).

A pair's two coordinates are not ports: the port is its challenge `z`, and the calls' words are `y_0(z)`, `y_1(z)`
(`CallWord::Pair`, §12 item 8).

**Not the alternative.** `crates/membership/quotient` adds the public weight inside `h`'s identity:
`(h + weight·w)·d·d'`. Here that would have raised π2v1's `q` to `5N` (an `18N`-word table, `2^28.17`). π2v1 kept the
identity at `5N` with the outside tags in the copy argument; π2v2's `T` is below `2N`, inside its `3N`.

The public data is in π2's stream, bound by its first challenge (§4).

### 2.5 Sizes against `2^28`

| commitment | content | words | stack, list |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `W` (proof) | `w`: `2^24` `K`-words | `2^24` | `2^24`, 9,952 |
| `Q` (proof, after `α, β, γ`) | `h` (`3N`), `q` in 2 chunks (`6N`), `P` (`N`), `q_P` (`2N`) | `2^27.58` (25% under `2^28`; π2v1 `15N`, `2^27.91`) | `2^28`, 3,564 |
| setup | `κ`, `κ + σ` | `2^25` | `L = 1` |

### 2.6 D32v: the other layout

**Rows = slices.** One row per `(round, z)`, 32 positions:

- pulls: `b_j[·][z]` (25) and `C_{j+1}[·][z-1]` (5);
- pushes: 25 expressions `a'_{j+1} = A_j + C[x-1] + C_prev[x+1]` (with `A_j = χ(b) + RC`, `C = Σ_y A_j`), and 5
  `C`'s.

There are no forwards in Keccak at all. Per permutation, 25 × 64 × 32 = 51,200 positions, a θ-prep slice row
included. The verifier gets 32 fold values and `ξ` (33 values). The compiler builds it (`Layout::D32v`; rounds as in
§10.4 item 7) and the PIOP proves it (`PI2_LAYOUT=d32v`), but its push side is several times D16v's (§10.3), and it has
not been measured on the Keccak VM's π1.

## 4. π2's protocol

The protocol as built is `PI2.md` §2. In outline:

- **Setup** (constants of the contract): the root of the setup (`κ`, `κ + σ`); π2's IV,
  `keccak("leanvm-rec-iv-v2" ‖ π1's contract IV ‖ the setup root ‖ the circuit's digest ‖ the three layout words)`
  (`prover::iv`); π1's setup root `F` and π1's layout words, which π1's calls need.
- **Public inputs:** the public input (32 B), π1's roots `C1`, `C2`, π1's claims' elements (14 `z_j`, 3 values).
- **The transcript** (Keccak-256, on chain, `KeccakChannel`'s shape):
  1. the seed `keccak("leanvm-rec-pi2v2" ‖ IV ‖ public input)`; the public data in the stream, absorbed by the first
     challenge;
  2. `W`'s root, then `α`, `β`, `γ`;
  3. `Q`'s root, then `t`;
  4. the row's 16 fold values `a_k`, `ξ = κ(t) + βσ(t)` and `s = P(t)` (18 values); then `ρ`.
- **The verifier**, at `t`: the digits; the row's cells from the `a_k`; the (block, role) indicators; the internal
  pushes `P_v(t)`; the ports' `Φ` and their term; `d_pull`, `d_push = α + s + ξ`; the identity and `P`'s definition,
  all as fractions with one inversion at the end.
- **Claims** (4 in 3 calls): `W` at `(ρ, ρ², ρ⁴, ρ⁸, w_4(t), …, w_23(t))`; `Q`, the identity's combination of `h(t)` and
  `q(t)`, `P(t) = s` and `P`'s definition in one claim (at `(u_0, …, u_24, ρ', ρ, ρ² B, y_0, y_1)`), and the sum
  `Σ_V h = 0` at the top coefficient's point; the setup at `(w_0(t), …, w_23(t), β)`, value `ξ`.
- **The contract**, in order: reads the stream; runs π2's verifier; checks π1's 3 claims declared (with
  π1's roots, layout words and setup root); then π2's (`W`, the setup, `Q`).

## 5. Soundness (modelled; the Lean bounds)

Each challenge's error is its Schwartz–Zippel bound times the lists of the commitments it is drawn against (WHIR
takes no OOD sample at level 0). The setup is honest, so `L = 1`. `params/leanvm_rec_params.py` prints these rounds;
an adversary of `Q` permutation queries breaks a layer with probability about `Q (ε_WHIR + ε_round)`, its openings
charged once at `269 · 2^-137 = 2^-128.93` a query and `ε_round` its weakest round after the lists
(`docs/leanvm/univariate/SPEC.md` §9).

**π2's rounds**, as the Lean states them (`formal/RecPiop`'s `err`), at `N = 2^24` over the stacks of §2.5:

| round | error | alone | after the lists |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| the copy argument `(α, β, γ)`: `2N` fractions, the ports' poles (`PI2.md` §7) and the three four-limb ports' fingerprints (degree 1 in `γ`), over `W`'s list 9,952 | `(4N + 3)/2^192` | 166.0 | 152.7 |
| `t`, the identity and `P`'s definition, each of degree `< 3N`, over `W`'s and `Q`'s lists (9,952 · 3,564 = `2^25.1`) | `(3N − 1)/2^192` | 166.4 | **141.3** |
| `ρ`, `W`'s fold (degree 15), over both lists | `15/2^192` | 188.1 | 163.0 |
| `ρ`, `Q`'s claim (degree 3), over both lists | `3/2^192` | 190.4 | 165.3 |

These are π2v2's; the Lean (`formal/RecPiop`'s `err`) states π2v1's: the copy argument `(3N + 2)/2^192` (153.1), `t`
the identity of degree `< 5N` (140.6), `ρ` (163.0).

With its three openings once, π2 keeps **128.93 bits** as `Q/ε` at `Q = 2^64`: `RecCompose.pi2Bound`, which
`formal/RecCompose` proves (`pi2Bound_le_bits`), is this bound.

**π1-rec3's rounds** are the main line's (`docs/leanvm/univariate/SPEC.md` §9, at 128 signatures, `C1`'s list 4,473
and `C2`'s 6,296), except:

- the bus `(α = (c, c², c⁴, c⁸), β)`: degree 16 a fingerprint factor (§1.4): 154.3 bits after `C1`'s list;
- Flock's eq point as powers: degree `2^22 − 1` over its 22 derived coordinates (§1.4): 157.9;
- the batch: a pair of degree 8 with a narrow challenge errs with probability `8/2^160`, a plain round `2/2^160`,
  over `C1`'s and `C2`'s lists (`SPEC-POINTS.md` §5): **132.3**, π1-rec3's weakest round (`t` is 143.2).

With its three openings once, π1-rec3 keeps 128.79 bits as `Q/ε`; the Lean bound
(`UniKeccakSound.uniKnowledgeSoundnessKeccakRec3_closed`) is 128.49, its rounds capped at `2^35/|E|` and multiplied by
9,952² (`docs/leanvm/univariate/SPEC.md` §9 explains the gap).

**The composition.** π2's knowledge soundness gives a π1-rec3 transcript on which π1's verifier (exactly its graph,
Keccak-256 bit for bit) outputs the claimed claims. The block proves those claims, declared, and π1's soundness
applies. The errors add: π2's, π1-rec3's and its merge hazard's (`2^16/|E|` a query, `RecGraphKeccak.hazard_probK`),
about **127.9 bits** as `Q/ε` at `Q = 2^64` with the calculator's π1-rec3 rounds, 127.7 with the Lean's. The Lean
statement of this composition is `formal/RecCompose` (`formal/README.md`, "The recursion's end theorems"), with
π1-rec3's two terms at the real Keccak-f[1600].

**Fiat–Shamir.** Both layers are random-oracle Fiat–Shamir over Keccak-256. A cheating prover can grind π1's
challenges offline, as it could on chain: the per-query bounds above hold, with `Q` queries.

smaller `N`.

### 10.4 Decisions and deviations from §2.3–§2.6

1. **The balancing is automatic.** Each value is placed at its readers: forwards, and at most one expression slot
   (its end).
   - A value with no slot placement ends at a **free end**: a slot whose pull nothing places and no formula of its
     row reads.
   - A slot whose push nothing asks for, but whose pull is placed, pushes **junk**.
   - Every non-port slot produces one value and every placed slot ends one. So the values without an end and the
     free ends are equally many, and any matching closes every chain.
   - Examples: the last call's unused lanes, round 23's `C_24`, and bits read only by forwards.
   - A slot with neither a placed pull nor a requested push is **dead**: a self-loop of 0, and its formula must
     vanish on its row.
   - The invariant, checked by `Builder::finish`:
     - a value is placed at most at one slot, and a free value at none;
     - every cell a live formula reads is placed (an end is read by its row; nothing live reads a free end or an
       unplaced forward);
     - every placed cell is read by some formula of its row.
2. **The public side has holes.** A push to an outside tag leaves its position's tag unpushed: `σ` has as many holes as
   ports. The holes are the ports' own positions; a port is the last role of a port row, and the port rows are blocks,
   so the holes are cosets. The PIOP leaves the holes out of the pulls (`PI2.md` §4.1): a hole's value is
   unconstrained, which is harmless because no formula reads a hole (`pi2::circuit::Circuit::validate`, asserted by
   `Spec::of` and `Setup::new`).
3. **The row counts are not public.** They are stream bytes hashed by π1's transcript, and nothing on chain reads
   them. The ports are the statement (4 limbs), the 2 roots (4 limbs), the calls' distinct elements and the zero
   checks (3 limbs); the calls' constant words stay constants.
4. **ι by `κ`:** `κ` is allowed at any expression slot, and the formulas have no constant term.
   - D16v: `κ = RC_j[z]` at `k0` roles 0 and 5 (no split of plane 0 by `RC`).
   - D32v: at the 7 roles whose push holds `A[0,0]`.
5. **D16v's Keccak-f is 130,944 positions a permutation** (the design: 130,736).
   - `kprep` is 160 rows: two `C_0` units of 5 forwards and a sum, and two D units. The input ends at round 0's χ
     slot, so its `kprep` reads are forwards.
   - `kd` is 344 rows, with the D units drawn from a pool (`k0`'s, `kprep`'s, then `kd`'s).
   - Every round's rows are made first.
6. **Constants live in the Keccak rows' spare roles**: D16v's `k1` role 15; D32v's roles 30–31 and the last round's
   25–31. A spare is an expression slot pushing 0, dead when unused. So there is no constants region.
7. **D32v's rounds:** `prep` (θ of the input), and `prep-first` for a first block, whose capacity lanes are not
   read. Then 23 `round`s (χ, ι, the next θ), and `last` (χ and ι only).
8. **`E` units:**
   - The operand that ends at the unit is the one with fewer uses left.
   - A unit where neither operand can end (each ends once) forwards both and has three more slots: 9 positions
     instead of 6.
   - Squares are products with `b = a`.
   - The limbs of a statement word, integer or bit field are fresh zero constants beyond the low one.
9. **Packings** are Horner by 15-bit windows from the top: `pack` is `acc·x^15 + Σ b_i x^i`, and `pack-top4` is a
   64-bit limb's top 4 bits. Absent bits read a shared zero, and a wider top window starts from a fresh zero.
10. **Free bits:** every bit is a self-loop with a copy (one region). A bit at a first block's slot uses the copy;
    the others leave it junk.
11. **Reads never hashed** (the batch's 3 end values, 9 limbs) are **free values**: cycles of forwards, any word,
    bound by the ports.
12. **Blocks are padded** (`layout::pad_counts`). A region of `c` rows padded to `k` binary digits is `k` aligned
    blocks, so fewer digits mean fewer selector pairs. Each region starts at one digit; while the padded total exceeds
    the power of two the unpadded rows need, the region whose next step saves the most rows per added digit (per
    expression role) takes it. The step **looks past digit counts that save nothing**: `keccak16/k1` at 400 signatures
    (118 × 6,144 = 724,992 rows) pads to 786,432 at both 2 and 3 digits and saves 61K rows from 3 to 4, so a one-digit
    greedy restored every small region's digits first. With the look-ahead the Keccak π1 at 400 signatures is 22
    blocks and 144 selector pairs; the one-digit greedy gave 70 and 429 (47,791 of execution, 63,423 of transaction
    gas). Port regions are not padded.
13. **The witness generator evaluates the circuit's own formulas**, from the free inputs (bits, never-hashed limbs).
    `eval::evaluate` is the reference it is checked against (every digest and element), rather than its source.

## 12. π1-rec3 and the optimisations

Each item says whether it touches π1's Fiat–Shamir shape or claims, π2's, or only the contract. The π2 side is
detailed in `PI2.md` §8–§9.

1. **One claim encoding** (`machine.rs`'s `root word, layout word, k, k × (point, value)`) for π1's and π2's claims,
   and **π1's IV binds its variant** (`contract_iv` hashes `variant_bytes`: the tag `"keccak-vm"`, the skip and the
   Fiat–Shamir shape's code).
2. **π2's values are 17** (π2's claims; 18 in π2v2, with `s = P(t)`). `ξ = κ(t) + βσ(t)` replaces `σ(t)`, `κ(t)`
   (the setup committed as `κ`, `κ + σ`, its claim at `β`); `h(t)` is not sent, `Q`'s claim being the identity's
   combination of `h(t)` and `q(t)`, with one inversion at the end.
3. **The fold's siblings** (π2's verifier only). The row's cells fold with half the products (`ω_{2k+1} = ω_{2k} + 1`).
4. **No claims on `A0`, `B0`** (π1's claims). Flock's lincheck terminal is computed by π1's verifier from the Keccak-f
   block's structure (`keccak_flock::structured_terminal`, about 3K `E` operations), so the matrices are never
   committed and π1's claims are `C1`, `C2`, `F` at the batch's point.
5. **The claims' words written once** (the contract only: `evm_link`'s `reuse`; π2's claim order `W`, setup, `Q`).
6. **The zero checks' coset in closed form** (π2's circuit: the zero ports first, padded to a power of two; π2v1's
   verifier). π2v2's verifier reads no zero port at all (`PI2.md` §1, §9 lever 3c).
7. **Narrow batch challenges** (**π1's Fiat–Shamir shape**: `FsShape::Rec2` is `Rec` with them, a step of `Rec3`).
   The batching sumcheck's challenges are the hash's low 160 bits (`Channel::sample_narrow`, `record::Node::Narrow`),
   so each challenge has four zero bytes in calldata.
8. **The batch two variables a round** (**π1's Fiat–Shamir shape**: `FsShape::Rec3`; `SPEC-POINTS.md`). 14 narrow
   challenges for the point's coordinates; π2's ports bind the 14 challenges and the contract derives the coordinates
